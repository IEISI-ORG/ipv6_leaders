# IPv6 for Leaders
## Workshop Presentation Plan

### 1. The IPv6 Basics in Business Terms
- What IPv6 is (the next generation internet protocol) and why it matters
- The exhaustion of IPv4 addresses and business implications
- Key differences from IPv4 that matter to decision-makers
- **Flipping from the scarcity to abundance mindset** in network address planning

### 2. Business Case for IPv6 Adoption
- Future-proofing your digital infrastructure
- Competitive advantages of early adoption
- Cost implications of delaying vs. implementing now
- **The Growth Problem**
  - IPv4 scarcity as a barrier to internet growth and new market entrants
  - Rising costs of IPv4 addresses affecting developing regions
  - Address sharing limitations and service quality impacts
  - Connection between IPv6 adoption and digital inclusion

### 3. Security Considerations in IPv6 Transitions
- Debunking the myth: Why IPv6 is not inherently more secure than IPv4
- Security constants: Fundamental security practices that remain unchanged
- Beyond NAT: Security architecture when network address translation is removed
- Critical firewall and security controls in IPv6 environments
- New attack vectors and security considerations specific to IPv6
- Security governance and risk management during transition periods
- Developing a comprehensive security strategy for IPv6 deployment

### 4. Tracking Adoption: Insights from APNIC Labs
- Understanding national and regional adoption trends
- How to interpret APNIC Labs statistics and dashboards
- Benchmarking your organization against industry and geographic averages
- Using adoption metrics to inform your timeline and strategy

### 5. IPv6 and Sustainable Development Goals
- Supporting SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) through expanded connectivity
- Enabling SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) via smart city applications
- Contributing to SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) by bridging the digital divide
- Facilitating SDG 13 (Climate Action) through more efficient network design
- Case studies of IPv6 deployments supporting sustainability initiatives globally

### 6. The Multi-Stakeholder Model and IPv6 Governance
- Understanding the multi-stakeholder Internet governance framework
- Key players in IPv6 governance: RIRs, ICANN, IETF, and their roles
- How IPv6 adoption influences policy development and governance structures
- Opportunities for organizational participation in technical governance
- Regional and national IPv6 task forces and their policy impact
- Balancing government, private sector, and civil society interests in IPv6 deployment

### 7. Common Adoption Barriers and Solutions
- Technical complexity and knowledge gaps
- Budget constraints and ROI calculations
- Legacy systems compatibility
- Organizational resistance to change

### 8. What to Look for in an IPv6 Address Plan
- Strategic allocation principles for business growth
- Security considerations in address design
- Departmental and functional organization strategies

### 9. Evaluating a Deployment Plan
- Key milestones and timeline expectations
- Risk management approaches
- Staff training and resource allocation
- Dual-stack vs. other transition mechanisms

### 10. Measuring Success and ROI
- Key performance indicators for IPv6 deployment
- Methods for calculating return on investment
- Long-term value metrics beyond technical implementation
- Case studies of successful value creation through IPv6
- Dashboard creation for executive visibility into progress

### 11. Future-Proofing: Beyond Basic IPv6 Adoption
- Preparing for the Internet of Things and massive device scaling
- Edge computing considerations and IPv6 deployment
- 5G/6G networks and their reliance on IPv6
- Emerging technologies that will depend on IPv6 capabilities
- Strategic visioning for fully-connected business operations

### 12. Learning & Development Strategy for IPv6
- Building an IPv6 competency framework for your organization
- Navigating external training resources: APNIC Academy, NSRC, RIPE NCC Academy
- Leveraging free and low-cost NGO resources (Internet Society, IETF, regional NOGs)
- Creating internal knowledge sharing mechanisms
- Developing executive-to-technical translation capabilities
