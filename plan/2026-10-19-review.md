## B. Content accuracy

### B1. Adoption statistics are seven months old — P1
Both decks cite APNIC Labs March 2026. Live figures (APNIC Labs, 30-day window to 2026-10-17):

| Economy | On slides | Live |
|---|---|---|
| World | ~43% | 43.6% |
| India | ~78% | 76.4% |
| France | ~86% | 85.7% |
| Germany | ~74% | 72.7% |
| USA | ~59% | 62.1% |
| Australia | ~44% | 47.6% |
| New Zealand | ~30% | 33.0% |

Direction of each claim still holds. Update the numbers and the "Last updated" line, and re-run `make images` so the five screenshot slides match. The Google "~47%" figure could not be checked (JavaScript-only page).

### B2. The Pacific headline numbers are almost entirely Starlink — the "early stage" line is right, but needs the evidence — P1
`ipv6-board-briefing.md:147`, `ipv6-marp-slide-deck.md:239`. Economy-level APNIC Labs figures look strong for several Pacific economies (Kiribati 57%, Nauru 53%, Tuvalu 50%, Cook Islands 41%), but the per-network breakdown shows where that IPv6 comes from. Share of each economy's IPv6-capable samples carried by Starlink (AS14593), APNIC Labs, 30 days to 2026-10-17:

| Economy | Starlink share of IPv6 | Domestic operators |
|---|---|---|
| Tuvalu, Cook Islands, Vanuatu | 100% | 0% IPv6-capable |
| Kiribati, Nauru, FSM, Samoa | 99–100% | ATH, Digicel, FSMTC, Vodafone Samoa: 0% |
| Solomon Islands, Marshall Islands | 96% | SATSOL 18% capable; NTA 0% |
| Tonga | 48% | Tonga Communications 29% capable (the other 52%) |
| PNG | 12% | Digitec 51% capable carries 88% of PNG's IPv6 |
| Fiji | 5% | Vodafone Fiji 60% capable carries 94% of Fiji's IPv6; Telecom Fiji 5% |

So the correct reading is: outside Fiji, PNG and (partly) Tonga, domestic Pacific operators have deployed effectively no IPv6; the IPv6 that APNIC Labs sees is Starlink terminals. "Pacific Islands: early stage" is accurate for the domestic operators the workshop is aimed at. The economy-level percentages on their own would mislead a board in the opposite direction.

Recommendation: keep the "early stage" line, but add one slide with the table above. It makes three points leaders will feel: an outside provider is already delivering IPv6 to your citizens while the national operator delivers none; Fiji and PNG show it is doable by a domestic operator; and the gap is measurable per network, so a board can ask "what is our ASN's number?". Replace "first-mover opportunity" with "first domestic mover" to be exact.

Caveat: APNIC Labs samples come from online ads, so the per-economy sample sizes are small (Vanuatu 229 IPv6 samples; Nauru, Tuvalu under 2,000). Direction is unambiguous; quote the shares as rounded figures.

### B3. "Built-in security with IPsec" contradicts the deck's own security section — P1
`ipv6-marp-slide-deck.md:92` lists IPsec as a key difference "that matters to leaders". Slide 20 then debunks "IPv6 is inherently more secure". IPsec has been SHOULD, not MUST, since RFC 6434 (2011) and RFC 8504. Remove the bullet or reword to "Same security tools and standards apply".

### B4. IPv4 price forecasts are presented to boards as fact — P1
`ipv6-board-briefing.md:100,128,132,297`: "~$20 (2025) → below $10 (2026–27) → economically worthless (2028)"; "Dual-stack operational costs will exceed IPv4 utility by 2028". We are now inside the forecast window. Label these as IEISI projections, add a market data source alongside the Medium article (IPv4.Global or Hilco Streambank auction data), and add the 2026 actual price. Current broker prices could not be verified in this session (broker sites block scripted access); do this check by hand.

### B5. "75 billion IoT devices by 2030" is a superseded 2016 projection — P1
`ipv6-marp-slide-deck.md:603`. Current analyst projections are roughly half that figure. Replace with a sourced current number or drop the number and keep the point.

### B6. Transition mechanisms slide includes deprecated 6to4 — P2
`:523`. 6to4 was deprecated by RFC 7526 (2015). For leaders, simplify to dual-stack, IPv6-mostly (RFC 8925), and IPv6-only with NAT64/DNS64 and 464XLAT. Drop 6to4/6in4/6rd.

### B7. "Every serious security framework — NIST, ISO 27001, CIS — requires firewalls, not NAT" — P2
`ipv6-board-briefing.md:211`. ISO 27001 does not name firewalls. The blog's wording ("require organisations to govern what's on their network", `ipv6-boardroom-blog.md:29`) is accurate; use it.

### B8. Unsourced forward claims — P2
- "universal adoption is projected for the mid-2040s" (`ipv6-board-briefing.md:148`; blog attributes it to APNIC data). Add the source on the slide.
- "147 meta-analyses" is cited to ISOC Pulse (secondary). Cite the primary (Albarracín et al., Nature Reviews Psychology, 2024) if you want it to survive a sceptical board member.
- Blog: "The US federal government has mandated IPv6-only for new infrastructure". Cite OMB M-21-07.

### B9. RIR exhaustion dates — OK
IANA Feb 2011, APNIC Apr 2011, RIPE NCC Sep 2012, LACNIC Jun 2014, ARIN Sep 2015 are all correct. AFRINIC is omitted; add it only if you want the full set.
